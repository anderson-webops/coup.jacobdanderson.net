The rulebook model

Claims, challenges, and costs

Every game uses the standard five-role, fifteen-card court deck. Two influence cards are dealt to each player.

01

One action per turn

Income gives 1 coin, Foreign Aid gives 2, Tax claims Duke for 3, and a Coup costs 7. A player starting a turn with at least 10 coins must Coup.

02

Claim a role or bluff

Assassin pays 3 to attack, Captain steals up to 2, and Ambassador exchanges with the court. A truthful claim survives a challenge and still resolves after the challenger loses influence.

03

Blocks can be challenged

Duke blocks Foreign Aid, Contessa blocks assassination, and Captain or Ambassador blocks stealing. A proven block holds; a disproved block fails.

04

The Assassin's fee

A successful Contessa block leaves the 3-coin fee spent. A successful challenge to the Assassin claim cancels the action and refunds the fee if the actor remains in play.

Rules checked against the base Coup rulebook PDF and publisher-maintained Dized rules.

What the bots actually do

Policy definitions

These are transparent heuristics. Every policy coups at 7 coins; the rulebook requires a coup only at 10. After two failed actions, a policy takes guaranteed Income once.

Baseline

Claims only held roles. Prioritizes Duke tax, then Captain steals, occasional Ambassador exchanges, and Foreign Aid. Challenges impossible claims.

Income

Takes guaranteed Income until a Coup is affordable.

Foreign Aid

Seeks 2 coins per turn, subject to Duke blocks.

Taxer

Claims Duke tax on every non-Coup turn, including as a bluff.

Captain

Claims a steal whenever another player has coins, whether or not it holds Captain.

Assassin

Claims assassination at 3 or more coins and uses Foreign Aid to fund it.

Exchange

Sometimes exchanges when holding Ambassador without Duke; otherwise follows the truthful baseline.

Bluff heavy

Claims Tax without checking its hand and claims blocks whenever possible.

Challenge low

Uses baseline actions; sometimes challenges Assassin, Captain, or Contessa claims.

Challenge heavy

Uses baseline actions and challenges every role claim.

Block aggressive

Uses baseline actions but claims any available block, even without the card.

Evidence and limits

How to read these numbers

The simulator rotates a distinct focal player through every seat. Each candidate in the same matchup uses the same initial seed panel; mixed opponents cycle across positions.

Win rate

Wins divided by all scheduled games, including games that reached the 1,000-action cap. Draws have their own column.

Uncertainty

Table intervals are 95% Wilson intervals for a policy's win rate. Finding cards use paired differences for the four preselected top-two pairs, with a simultaneous 95% correction.

Scope

Policies use only private cards and public information. The model omits negotiation, optional variants, and the choice to decline proof of a held role. Response priority is clockwise when several players could act.

Two players

The first seat starts with one coin under the base two-player rule. View those exploratory results separately from standard multiplayer games.

Reproduce or inspect

The source, per-game outcomes, seat rows, study manifest, and rule citations are kept together. Prior invalid CSVs are clearly archived outside the active results.
